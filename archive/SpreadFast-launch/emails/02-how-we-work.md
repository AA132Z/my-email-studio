Last week we said we start with your category. Here's what that looks like.

A category study shows how businesses like yours usually make and lose money. We use advanced analytics, which means we look for patterns in the sales, costs and customers of your category. Then we compare those patterns with your own numbers.

That comparison lets us estimate the size of the opportunity: roughly how much money is at stake. **It's an estimate, not a promise,** and we always tell you where it comes from.

Here are three samples. **They are made up.** They are not real clients, and the numbers are only there to show how it works.

- **Sample restaurant.** Sales held steady, but food costs and delivery-app fees went up. In this sample, we'd estimate about $3,000 a month in margin is at stake. First thing we'd check: the cost of each of the top ten dishes.
- **Sample clothing shop.** Some sizes sell out fast, while others sit on the rack for months. In this sample, we'd estimate about $2,500 a month in profit is at stake from stock that doesn't sell. First thing we'd check: sales by size and by season.
- **Sample online store.** Lots of orders, but many come from heavy discounts and one costly ad channel. In this sample, we'd estimate about $4,000 a month in profit is at stake. First thing we'd check: profit by channel, not just sales.

Your numbers will be different. That's why we look at your data before we say anything about your business.
